Reviewer 1 Comments to the Author

This work investigates wavelength-dependent PPC in MoS2 and its application to photonic synapse and physical reservoir computing. In MoS2 back-gate device, the shorter wavelength (blue light) produces longer PPC lifetime, while the longer wavelength (red light) leads to the shorter lifetime. According to authors, the higher photon energy can selectively activate deep-level traps, while lower energy photons can activate only shallow-level traps, supported by the temperature-dependent PPC measurement. For the photonic synapse applications, they observed that the shorter wavelength leads to long-lasting PPF index per different pulse-to-pulse delay, but the accuracy in the MNIST classification was similar for all wavelengths. For the physical reservoir computing, authors used the prediction of non-linear equation as benchmark test. Here the input was given as light pulses, and the changes of PPC captured after each light pulse were used as virtual nodes. The wavelength-dependent PPC lifetime was used to improve the accuracy of the physical reservoir computing. Compared to a single wavelength case, when all three wavelengths were associated, the prediction accuracy was highly improved, which confirms the benefit of wavelength-dependent PPC.  
The paper introduces interesting behavior of PPC in MoS2 devices, and demonstrated timely application in PRC. However, the manuscript in its current form also suffers from multiple weaknesses which should be improved. The reviewer suggests authors to address the following points.  
  
1. For the device, additional characterizations are required, such as thickness of the channel, Raman/PL spectroscopy, etc. Moreover, the reported results seem to be from a single device. Is there any **device-to-device variation**? Results from multiple devices are highly recommended.  
2. The equation for the PPC (Eq. 4) consists of multiple time constants, corresponding to shallow and deep traps. However, **no fitting to the experimental data** is shown. Fitting results and corresponding analysis should be provided. Additionally, if there are any similar cases, **comparison with existing reports** will be beneficial.  
3. The significance of the prediction accuracy in PRC should be provided compare to other PRC study. A table comparing this work with other state-of-the-art is recommended. Also, the result seems to be derived from a single best case. Can authors provide deeper analysis with statistical information, such as prediction accuracy over different train/test sets, different regression types, various combinations of physical nodes such as n = 2 (two devices), etc?  

Reviewer 2 Comments to the Author

This work presents a optoelectronic synaptic transistor based on mechanically exfoliated MoS2. The author systematically investigates its persistent photoconductivity and synaptic plasticity under illumination of three wavelengths, namely red (660 nm), green (520 nm), and blue (440 nm). The device is further successfully applied to wavelength-multiplexed physical reservoir computing. The work is complete, logically clear, and possesses certain innovativeness. Therefore, I recommend the publication of this manuscript. The authors need to consider the following minor revisions before the manuscript is acceptable for publication.  
Comment #1: Many studies have reported the application of MoS2 in optoelectronic devices. What are the advantages of your work compared with previously reported research?  
Comment #2: The abstract needs to be revised to highlight the significance of this study.  
Comment #3: Material characterizations are insufficient. To enhance the rigor of the conclusions, please supplement additional measurements such as Raman spectroscopy and XPS.
